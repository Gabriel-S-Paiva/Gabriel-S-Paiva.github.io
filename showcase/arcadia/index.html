What it is

Arcadia is a recycling game for the Computer Graphics course at ESMAD. In the first phase you run and jump through a level collecting waste: paper, glass, plastic and general waste. In the second phase the camera turns on and you sort what you collected into the right recycling bins with hand gestures.

The name mixes three ideas: Arcadia, the idyllic region of ancient Greece; the arcade (arcada) that is a landmark of the ESMAD campus; and arcade games.

Who built what

The repo is public, so the split is stated up front. Everything under js/Classes/ (Player, Platform, Box, Goal, Residuos, Vector) is mine, and so is the game loop, the camera and the level layout in game.js. On the game page I also built the collection counter, the instructions modal and the victory popup.

My teammate built the second phase: hand tracking with MediaPipe Hands, gesture recognition and the drag-to-bin sorting. They also built the main menu, the settings, credits and instructions pages, and the site-wide styling.

How the two phases meet

The phases are separate pages, so they needed a contract. I kept it small: when the player reaches the goal, the counts per waste type go into localStorage, and the gesture phase reads them back.

Physics without an engine

The player is a point with velocity and acceleration, integrated with explicit Euler every frame. Gravity is 1,200 px/s², horizontal input is a force capped at 300 px/s, and when no key is held a drag term slows the player down. Frame time is clamped to 50 ms, so one long frame cannot move anything far enough to skip through a platform.

Collision is axis-aligned boxes. When the player overlaps a platform, the smaller overlap decides the axis: push out sideways, or land on top, or hit your head. Three kinds of platform share that code:

  • Solid platforms block from every side.
  • One-way platforms can be jumped through from below. A landing only counts if the player’s feet were above the platform on the previous frame and the player is not moving up relative to it.
  • Moving platforms carry the player. On landing, the player takes on the platform’s velocity, so riding one does not jitter.

Boxes use the same overlap test. Pushing sideways hands half the player’s speed to the box and slows the player, which is what makes a crate feel heavy. Boxes also fall, land on platforms and stack on each other.

Camera and debug view

The level is 2,016 px wide, wider than the canvas. The camera has a dead zone in the middle 40% of the screen and only moves when the player leaves it, easing toward the target instead of snapping.

Pressing D draws every hitbox, the grid and the live player state over the game.

What I would change

  • Fixed timestep for the physics.
  • One collision routine shared by the player and the boxes. Right now the overlap math is written twice.
  • Name the phase handoff: a small module that owns the keys, instead of string literals on both sides.
